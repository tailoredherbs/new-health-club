Wellness is moving deeper into the real estate stack.

Recent projects are not only adding gyms, spas or cold plunges to premium buildings. They are using wellness brands and operating systems to shape the identity of residential, hospitality and mixed-use assets.

In Bali, Nirvana Life is presenting The Sanctuary Hotel Suites as a part-ownership hospitality product connected to its Canggu wellness club. Body Factory has also extended its brand into lifestyle residence projects, including Canggu and Uluwatu.

In the United States, The WELL is entering residential real estate in Miami, while Kaliʻu is integrating resident-only recovery technologies such as robotic massage, multimodal recovery chambers and flotation bathing into a condo project.

At the global luxury end, Clinique La Prairie and SHA Wellness Clinic are expanding through longevity hubs, resorts, branded residences and destination hospitality projects.

The commercial logic is clear. Wellness gives property a stronger narrative, higher perceived value, recurring service revenue and a clearer lifestyle identity. It also gives residents and guests a reason to use the building beyond sleep, dining or occasional spa visits.

This shifts wellness from amenity to anchor.

The next phase will likely separate serious wellness-led real estate from projects using wellness as surface branding. The important distinction is whether the wellness layer changes the operating model, daily usage and revenue mix of the property — or whether it remains a marketing label attached to a standard luxury development.
