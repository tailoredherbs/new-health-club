# The New Health Club — Editorial Criteria

Canonical scope rules for Signals and Reports. This document doubles as the
selection criteria for the automated news pipeline (LLM filter prompt).

## The test

**Does this news change the map?**
Does it alter who operates where, what a venue offers, or how a premium client
would choose between venues? → In scope.
Is it about what people consume, wear, or do at home? → Out of scope.

## Ring 1 — Always publish (venue-level news)

News about specific wellness-led physical venues across all five Index categories
(Longevity Sanctuaries, Execution Hubs, Practitioner-Led Boutiques, Retreats, Clubs):

- Openings, announced projects, expansions to new cities/properties
- Closures, ownership changes, rebrands
- New programs, membership tiers, pricing-model changes
- Venue-level partnerships (e.g. a clinic operator entering a hotel/club)
- Leadership moves at venue or operator level (medical directors, founders, key hires)

Priority order when volume is high: venues already on the Index > premium peers
not yet mapped > notable mid-market only if it signals a model shift.

## Ring 2 — Selectively publish (the business layer)

Operator and market-structure news that shapes the venue landscape:

- Funding rounds, M&A, franchise programs of venue operators
- Hospitality groups building wellness arms; brand-led club concepts
- Wellness real estate and longevity residences
- Regulatory or scientific changes ONLY when they directly alter what venues
  can offer (frame through the venue impact, never as a science story)

These go to Signals; quarterly synthesis goes to Reports.

## Ring 3 — Excluded

- Consumer wellness products: supplements, wearables, apps, GLP-1 discourse
- Longevity science and research without a direct venue consequence
- Biohacking protocols, training methods, personal-optimization content
- Generic trend listicles and consumer-press roundups without venue news

## Voice

Signals are written as field intelligence: factual, specific, operator-literate.
State what happened, the numbers if public, and why it matters for the category.
1–3 short paragraphs. No hype adjectives, no press-release tone.
